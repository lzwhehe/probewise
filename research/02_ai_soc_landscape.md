**Patterns.** (a) Almost all products run their own queries and enrichment through vendor integrations; differentiation is in *what data they reach*. (b) Most are moving to an orchestrator-plus-specialists design (Torq, 7AI, AgentiX, Dropzone). (c) The more credible designs pair LLM reasoning with deterministic tools or playbooks (Intezer forensics, Google playbooks, Exaforce behavioral models). (d) No vendor publishes false-negative rates. The most-cited "accuracy" numbers are agreement with in-house analysts (CrowdStrike) or come from small vendor-co-branded studies (Dropzone/CSA). Microsoft's RCT is vendor-authored. Gartner's 2025 Hype Cycle put AI SOC agents near the Peak of Inflated Expectations, at 1–5% penetration ([Cynet summary](https://www.cynet.com/blog/gartner-hype-cycle-2025-cybersecurity-ai-assistants-and-ai-soc-agents); primary report paywalled).

## 3. Criticisms, benchmarks, failure modes

**Benchmarks**
- **CyberSOCEval** (Meta + CrowdStrike, [arXiv 2509.20166](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20166v2), 2025-09): malware analysis and TI reasoning tasks. Models are "far from saturating" it, and reasoning models gain less than they do on coding or math. Reported score ranges of ~15–28% (malware) and 43–53% (TI) come from [secondary coverage](https://cybersecuritynews.com/cybersoceval/) — **verify against the paper**.
- **ExCyTIn-Bench** (Microsoft, [arXiv 2507.14201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.14201); [repo microsoft/SecRL](https://github.com/microsoft/SecRL), 159★): agents query 57 Sentinel-style log tables; questions come from investigation graphs and receive step-level reward. Best reported reward is 0.606. Dataset size differs between paper versions (589 vs 7,542 questions).
- **CTIBench** ([NeurIPS 2024 D&B](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/5acd3c628aa1819fbf07c39ef73e7285-Abstract.html)): MCQ, CVE→CWE, CVSS, ATT&CK extraction, attribution. Attribution accuracy is low, and models systematically overestimate severity (per [Emergent Mind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/ctibench)).
- **SIABENCH** ([arXiv 2603.06422](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06422), 2026-03-06): 25 incident scenarios plus 135 triage alerts, 11 LLMs. On triage, GPT-5 scored 98% / 97.1% and GPT-4o-mini 75% / 51.4%; GPT-4o-mini caught only 64% / 46.7% of false positives. On incident analysis, the best model fully solved 8 of 25 scenarios (Claude-4.5-Sonnet). The best open model (DeepSeek-Reasoner) trailed the best closed model by ~32 points. Llama-3.1-8B failed 94.3% of the time, mostly by looping. Hallucination and wrong commands were among the top failure causes.
- **SecAlertBench** ([repo](https://github.com/Dxsssu/SecAlertBench)): 8,322 real alerts from 3 enterprise SOCs, 16 LLMs. Average TPR was 79.71% and average FPR **44.13%**; the authors conclude LLMs are not ready as standalone triage.
- **Meta-critique:** benchmark scores are pipeline-dependent. A single harness choice can shift a score by >80 points, and 9 of 10 models moved ≥3 ranks under a standardized harness ([arXiv 2609.08765](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08765), 2026-09-08).

**Prompt injection through log data**
- "Poisoning the Watchtower" ([arXiv 2605.24421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24421), 2026-05-23) studies attacker-controlled fields (user-agent, URL, username). Persona-hijack payloads suppressed 68% of malicious logs under a naive classifier. Context manipulation in summarization reached 96% success, still 38% with constrained output. Average success dropped from 26.6% to 11.8% under the best defense. Direct overrides scored 0%. The analyst model was gpt-4o-mini only.
- LevelBlue SpiderLabs showed agents could be steered to alter attack details, hide attacks, or invent decoy events ([blog](https://www.levelblue.com/blogs/spiderlabs-blog/rogue-ai-agents-in-your-socs-and-siems-indirect-prompt-injection-via-log-files)). Also see the DEF CON 34 poster "Poisoning the SOC" ([AI Village](https://aivillage.org/posters/poisoning-the-soc-prompt-injection-via-ingested-telemetry/)).
- A secondary summary ([NeuralTrust, 2026-10](https://neuraltrust.ai/blog/ai-soc-prompt-injection)) cites further numbers: 83.4% ASR undefended, Prompt Shield flagging 1 of 32 log payloads, and a Sygnia case in which an MDR summary described Mimikatz as "maintenance". **Not verified against primaries.**

**Measurement critiques.** Vendor "accuracy" is usually agreement with the vendor's own analysts or performance on a few scenarios. Speed claims ("550% faster", "30 min→60 s") do not measure missed threats. Microsoft's own RCT attributes most of the gain to *queue prioritization*, not verdict quality ([arXiv 2511.13860](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.13860)).

## 4. Gaps and unmet needs (design opportunities)

1. **Auditable, verifiable reasoning.** Google and AiSOC log reasoning and tool calls, but no product publicly shows that each claim in a verdict is **tied to a retrieved evidence record** and machine-checked. Hallucination and wrong commands remain top failure causes (SIABENCH).
2. **Missed-threat measurement.** No vendor publishes false-negative rates. Benchmarks show high FPR (SecAlertBench 44%) and weak FP recognition in small models. An open tool could ship a FN/FP evaluation harness (e.g., replaying labeled alerts and running ExCyTIn/SIABENCH-style tasks).
3. **Calibration to customer history.** Dropzone's context memory and FunnyWolf's knowledge extraction are early attempts. Calibrated confidence (the probability a verdict is correct, given *this* org's past dispositions) is rarely exposed. Wazuh-Autopilot's "confidence floor" assumes calibrated scores that nobody validates.
4. **Injection-robust data/instruction separation.** Log fields are attacker-controlled, and filters catch few payloads. Constrained/structured output helps but does not eliminate the risk (38% residual). Few products document any defense.
5. **Local / open models.** There is a large gap on deep investigation (~32 points in SIABENCH), but a smaller one on triage (9–11 points). That supports hybrid local-first triage with escalation. Data residency matters too (Chinese vendors are pushing distilled 7B models).
6. **Cost transparency.** No commercial vendor publishes per-alert cost. Community examples cite ~$0.001/alert for Haiku-based Wazuh triage (GitHub topic listing, **unverified**).
7. **Benchmark reproducibility.** Scores are pipeline-sensitive (arXiv 2609.08765), so any claim should ship with a fixed harness.
8. **Chinese vendors** publish 降噪率 and competition rankings but no methodology. There is room for an open, reproducible evaluation on Chinese-language SOC data.
